# Learning to evolve

> Focused version (618 words, about 4:00), alongside 2026-11-10-full. One idea: our
> social tools evolve with the whole app as the unit of selection; life sped up by moving evolution
> inward (smaller units, faster loops, cheaper failure); WE is built to make that climb. Forest and
> body, the limits of integration and the "next transition" are left out.
>
> Device: three rungs of life (recombination, sharing sideways, learning) are set up in §3 and each
> pays off in §5. Visual motif: one loop (try, test, keep) that starts huge and slow and shrinks
> with each rung, until it runs inside a single community.

## 1. It already evolves {#already-evolves}

Every social app you've ever used was an experiment.

Someone had an idea about how people should talk, share or decide, and built it. Millions tried it.
Most of those experiments died. A few survived, and the next ones copied what worked.

So our social tools already evolve. Try something, test it, keep what works. But look at what's
being tested.

## 2. Evolution by death {#by-death}

The unit is the whole app: everything in it, tested at once. The variations come from a handful of
teams. And the test is simple: does the company survive?

So a brilliant idea inside a failing app dies with it. A bad idea inside a winning app lives on.
Each turn of the loop takes years, and costs a company.

That's evolution at its crudest: whole things, tested by death.

## 3. How life sped up {#life-sped-up}

Life started out like that too. But the remarkable thing about its history is that evolution itself
kept getting better. Three steps show how.

First, recombination. When living things only copied themselves whole, two good ideas in separate
lineages could only compete, until one died out. Recombination let good ideas from different
lineages come together in one.

Second, sharing sideways. Bacteria learned to pass useful genes directly to strangers. An innovation
no longer had to wait for its owners to win. It could jump straight to whoever needed it.

Third, learning. With brains, an animal could try something within its own lifetime, and keep it if
it worked. A failed experiment no longer cost a life. Just a moment.

Each step made the loop smaller and faster. The units got smaller. Failure got cheaper. And
evolution moved inside.

## 4. The bottom rung {#bottom-rung}

Our social tools haven't taken any of these steps.

Their ideas can't combine, because each one is sealed inside its own app. They can't jump between
groups: a tool built for one community can't reach another without becoming a whole new product.
And a community can't learn within its own life. It gets the shape it's given, until the app changes
or dies.

The tools that shape how billions of people understand the world, and act together, still evolve
like the earliest life.

## 5. Climbing {#climbing}

WE is built to change that.

It's a shared, open core for social software: trusted building blocks, and clear rules for how
pieces fit together. On top of it, anyone can build cartridges: a voting tool, a rota, a forum, a
whole community app. And each of life's steps has a counterpart.

Recombination: cartridges can be built from pieces of other cartridges, so good ideas combine
instead of competing.

Sharing sideways: a cartridge made for one community can travel to any other that wants it, without
anyone moving anywhere.

Learning: a group can try a new way of making decisions this month, and keep it if it works. A failed
experiment costs one part, not a company.

The loop gets small enough, and fast enough, to run inside a single community.

## 6. Who selects {#who-selects}

Faster evolution isn't automatically better. What it produces depends on what does the selecting.

Today, what survives is whatever keeps a company alive. Here, it's whatever the communities living
with these parts choose to keep.

And because opening a social space is risky, you can open any cartridge and see exactly what it does
before you let it in, and the core holds the rules for what it's allowed to touch.

## 7. Why it matters {#why-it-matters}

These aren't just apps. They're the tools we think together with. They shape what we notice, what we
understand, and what we're able to do together.

If they can evolve, the way we think together can evolve too.

## 8. Learning to evolve {#close}

Life didn't just evolve. It learned how to evolve better.

Our social tools can learn that too.
